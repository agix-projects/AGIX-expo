# Sponsorship Acquisition Campaign
## AGIX Agribusiness & Agritech Expo 2027

**Purpose:** This document is the complete, execution-ready sponsorship acquisition campaign for the AGIX Agribusiness & Agritech Expo 2027 (Friday 9 – Saturday 10 April 2027, Amadeo Event Center, Ebeano Tunnel Road, Enugu, Nigeria). It covers prospect identification, segmentation and value propositions, the proposed tier structure, full outreach copy, presentation and negotiation materials, onboarding and post-event stewardship.

**Owner:** Virtual Marketing Assistant (autonomous) / **Human approval required for:** sponsorship pricing and the final tier structure, any contractual commitment or bespoke benefit, discounts, meeting attendance on behalf of AGIX Africa, and sign-off of the prospect list before first outreach.

---

## 1. Prospect Identification Approach

1. **Category mapping first:** identify companies whose customers or beneficiaries overlap with the Expo audience (farmers, cooperatives, agribusiness owners, processors, investors, policymakers).
2. **Sources:** corporate CSR/sponsorship pages, annual reports, published sponsorships of comparable Nigerian business and agricultural events (for identification only — no claims about those events), association memberships, LinkedIn (marketing, brand, corporate affairs and sustainability leaders), development-partner programme listings.
3. **Qualification screen:** (a) audience overlap; (b) evidence of past event or community sponsorship; (c) active Nigeria/West Africa presence or expansion intent; (d) reachable decision-maker.
4. **Prioritisation:** Tier A (must-win, named-champion approach), Tier B (strong fit, standard sequence), Tier C (opportunistic, light-touch).
5. **List size proposal:** [number per segment — to be approved]; suggested starting point 8–12 Tier A and 15–20 Tier B/C prospects per segment below.
6. **Human sign-off** of the full list before any outreach is sent.

## 8. Negotiation Preparation Notes

**Flexible (Assistant may discuss, human approves final):**
- Package composition — swapping benefits of comparable value within a tier;
- Payment scheduling (instalments) [terms — to be confirmed];
- In-kind components (media value, equipment, hospitality, airline services) as partial consideration, subject to valuation policy [to be approved];
- Activation formats (clinic, lounge, demo, competition) within venue constraints.

**Not flexible (no discussion without prior human instruction):**
- Event dates, venue and event name;
- Exclusivity already granted to another sponsor;
- Editorial independence of the conference programme (speaking slots are benefits, but content standards apply);
- Compliance requirements: no sponsor may be described publicly as confirmed until contract signature and human approval of the announcement.

**Approval boundaries:**
- Any price other than the approved rate card: human approval required before offer.
- Any discount, bundle or bespoke benefit: proposed internally first, offered only after written human approval.
- Contract issuance and signature: human only. The Assistant prepares drafts and coordinates.

## 9. Objection Handling

| # | Objection | Response |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Our sponsorship budget is already allocated." | "Understood — most budgets for 2026 are. The Expo is in April 2027, so this typically sits in the 2027 cycle, and decisions made now simply secure the better tiers. May I send the proposal so it can enter your 2027 planning at the right time?" |
| 2 | "This is a first edition — what's the proven audience?" | "A fair question, and we won't pretend otherwise: this is a first edition, and we do not quote numbers we cannot stand behind. What we can show is the full visitor-recruitment plan — the channels, the partners, the campaign calendar — and we will report campaign progress to committed sponsors before the event. First-edition partners also get founding-partner positioning and renewal priority that later entrants never can." |
| 3 | "How is this different from other agricultural events?" | "Three ways: it is squarely focused on the commercial agribusiness and agritech value chain rather than a general fair; it is anchored in Enugu, giving direct access to the South East's agricultural economy; and sponsor packages are built around engagement — speaking, clinics, activations — not just logo placement. I'd be glad to map the differences against any specific event you are comparing." |
| 4 | "The amount is too high." | "Rather than negotiate the number first, let's fit the package to the objective. If the [tier] level is beyond this year's scope, a category sponsorship concentrates your budget on one high-visibility asset. And where budget genuinely can't stretch, part-payment scheduling or an in-kind component may be possible [subject to approval]. What outcome are you buying — visibility, leads, or positioning?" |
| 5 | "We prefer to just take a stand, not sponsor." | "Exhibiting is absolutely available, and some partners do both. The difference is reach: a stand engages the people who walk past it; sponsorship puts [Company] in front of every attendee — on stage, in the programme, across the campaign — before, during and after the event. If lead generation is the goal, sponsorship plus a stand is the strongest configuration. Shall I price both options side by side [pricing — to be confirmed]?" |
| 6 | "Send the proposal and we'll come back to you." | "Of course — it will be with you today. Two things help me serve you properly: who else will review it, and when does your sponsorship committee next meet? I'll diarise a follow-up call for shortly after that, and if the answer is no, a quick no is genuinely welcome." |
| 7 | "We only sponsor events in Lagos/Abuja." | "That is exactly why this is interesting: your competitors are concentrated where you are, and the South East audience — farmers, agri-SMEs, processors — is under-served by major platforms. Being the [category] brand that showed up in Enugu is a differentiated story, and one this event is built to tell." |
| 8 | "What guarantees do we get?" | "Every contracted deliverable — logo placements, speaking slot, exhibition space, campaign inclusions — is written into the agreement and tracked in a deliverables schedule you can inspect at any time. What we will not do is guarantee attendance figures we cannot control; we commit instead to full transparency on the recruitment campaign and a complete post-event report against every deliverable." |

## 10. Onboarding Workflow

1. Contract signed (human-executed) → sponsor record created in tracker.
2. Welcome email sent within 24 hours (copy below).
3. Kick-off call scheduled within 7 days: confirm deliverables, gather brand assets, agree announcement timing [announcement requires human approval].
4. Brand assets received (logo vector, brand guidelines, boilerplate, approved descriptors).
5. Deliverables tracker populated and shared with sponsor.
6. Invoicing per contract [terms — to be confirmed]; payment monitored.
7. Announcement drafted → human approval → published and shared with sponsor.
8. Monthly sponsor update email until March 2027; weekly from mid-March 2027.
9. Pre-event briefing (logistics, staffing, activation schedule) [~2 weeks before event].
10. On-site servicing → post-event report within [14] days → appreciation letter → renewal conversation.
